## Honest failures (documented weaknesses)

- **bare-account** (account id): leaked 1 value(s). Bare digit strings with a label but no checksum are not recognised.
- **name-unprotected** (name): leaked 1 value(s). Names are only masked when the user lists them under Always protect; there is no name recogniser.
- **fullwidth-at** (adversarial): leaked 1 value(s). A full-width @ sign evades the email rule.
- **split-secret** (adversarial): leaked 1 value(s). A key split into fragments is not recognised: only complete key shapes are matched.

These are the cases where AIRLOCK's rules do not protect a value. They are why the product always shows you the exact outbound text before anything is sent, and why AIRLOCK never claims complete protection.

## What this does not show

- Real-world documents are messier than this corpus; the detectors rely on patterns, not understanding.
- Names and street addresses are only protected when listed or matched by a supported pattern.
- The corpus was written by the same people who wrote the detectors, so it is optimistic.
